# Reel Visual Style: how MzPreloved videos look and move

This style comes from a reference reel: a 27-second vertical talking-head video that never lets the frame sit still. Its own on-screen line sums up the rule: **change something every 2 to 3 seconds.**

Use this when you turn an idea or script into shots, captions and edit notes. Content-principles decides *what* is said; this decides *what the viewer sees* while it's said.

Like content-principles, treat these as a style to reason from, not a checklist to recite back to the user. Hand them shot lists and captions, not this document.

---

## 1. The frame refreshes every 2–3 seconds

Nothing in the reference holds for long. Every beat brings a new angle, a new distance, a new location or a graphic.

Ways to refresh the frame, from cheapest to most effort:
- **Punch in or out** on the same clip (digital zoom between a medium shot and a tight close-up).
- **Change the distance:** close-up face → waist-up → full body standing in the room.
- **Change the angle:** eye level at a desk, low angle lying on the sofa, slightly high looking down.
- **Change the location:** desk, sofa, standing in the middle of the room, against a wall.
- **Cut to a full-screen graphic** or a product close-up.

When writing a shot list, if two consecutive beats would use the same shot, change one of them. A long single take is only acceptable when a strong curiosity loop is carrying it.

## 2. Captions: a few words, big, in the middle of the frame

The reference never shows full subtitles. It shows **one to three keywords per beat**, large and centred, across the middle of the frame (roughly chest height, never covering the eyes).

- **Default caption:** white, heavy bold sans-serif, lowercase or sentence case, with a subtle shadow so it reads on any background.
- **Emphasis caption:** the key phrase of a beat switches to an **italic serif in the accent colour** (deep green for MzPreloved; see section 5). Give it a thin off-white outline or soft light glow so a dark green still reads over moody footage. Use this sparingly: about one beat in four. If everything is emphasised, nothing is.
- Pick the word that carries the meaning, not the filler. "further", "closer", "angle", "2 to 3 seconds", never "so", "and", "you know".
- Captions appear exactly when the word is spoken and disappear before the next one. No long-lingering text.

For MzPreloved, the natural caption words are the facts a buyer cares about: the price, the brand, the size, the condition, "worn twice", "original tag", "no flaws", "last one".

## 3. Act out the words

The speaker in the reference physically does what he says:
- Says "closer" while pushing a lens toward the camera.
- Says "further" while leaning back from it.
- Holds up a phone reading "BORING" instead of just saying it.
- Lies on a sofa for an "otherwise" beat, so the shot itself shows what boredom looks like.

When planning a shot, ask: **what can the hands or body do that makes this line visible?** For clothing, that is almost always the item itself:
- "Soft fabric" → hand squeezing and rubbing the fabric in a close-up.
- "Still has the tag" → tag pulled into frame toward the lens.
- "Fits true to size" → quick try-on cut, mirror or full-body.
- "Small mark here" → finger pointing at the flaw, in focus.
- "Only RM__" → the price on a phone screen or a card held up, like the "BORING" phone.

Props held toward the lens create depth and pull attention; use them.

## 4. One full-screen graphic as a pattern break

Once in the reel, the face disappears entirely for a full-screen motion graphic: a flat accent-colour background with simple white line art (a marionette on strings), with the caption in the emphasis style on top.

- Use **one** of these per video, around the middle, where attention tends to sag.
- Keep it to the brand palette: accent colour background, white or off-white line art, no stock-footage look.
- For MzPreloved the background is deep green (or maroon in a maroon video). This could be: a flat-lay of the item on the accent background, a simple line drawing of a hanger or price tag, or a before/after split.

## 5. Light and colour stay consistent across every shot

Locations change constantly, but the look doesn't:
- **Warm, moody, low-key lighting** from practical lamps; amber and purple/magenta accents in the background; never flat overhead light.
- Background has texture: window blinds, a sofa, shelves, hangers on a rail. Hangers on a rail suit a clothing shop especially well.
- The speaker wears the same outfit throughout, so the many cuts still feel like one video.
- **One accent colour** ties captions and graphics together. The reference uses deep red; MzPreloved uses:
  - **Deep green `#1F4D3A`**: the default brand accent for emphasis captions and full-screen graphics.
  - **Maroon `#6B1E2E`**: the alternative, for items where green clashes (green or olive clothing) or for a seasonal or festive feel.
- Use **one accent per video**, never both. Within a video, every emphasis caption and graphic uses the same one.
- Green sits well with the warm amber lighting; lean the background practicals amber rather than purple so the green captions don't fight a magenta cast.

## 6. Shooting setup (vertical, 9:16)

- Shoot vertical 1080×1920 (the reference is 9:16). Keep faces and items in the middle two-thirds; the top and bottom are covered by app UI.
- Set up two or three positions in the room before recording and move between them per beat rather than editing a single take.
- Film the product inserts (tag, fabric, flaw, fit) separately afterwards; they're the easiest cuts to drop in wherever the frame has held too long.

---

## How to hand this to the user

When asked to plan or script a reel, give them something they can film directly: a beat-by-beat list where each beat has **the spoken line, the shot (distance/angle/location or product insert), the on-screen caption word(s), and whether it uses the emphasis style**. Keep each beat to roughly 2–3 seconds of speech. Don't explain the style back to them unless they ask why.
